What to expect in 2026

The 2026 Medical Device Make-A-Thon brings interdisciplinary student teams together for a 72-hour sprint to conceptualize, design, and prototype solutions to a real clinical challenge; with workshops, mentorship, and fabrication resources along the way.

Below you'll find this year's speakers, workshop leaders, judges, and mentors, plus the full weekend schedule. The problem statement is revealed Thursday evening.

No prior medical device experience is required; just curiosity, initiative, and a willingness to build.

The Challenge

2026 Problem Statement

From Symptom to Solution

Create a device that minimizes mobility issues derived from neurodegenerative diseases to improve functional independence.

Neurodegenerative diseases progressively damage nerve cells in the brain and/or spinal cord, often affecting movement, coordination, balance, and independence. Examples include Parkinson's disease, ALS, and Huntington's disease.

Finding Design Opportunities

Symptom + Activity = Friction = Opportunity

Pick a symptom, find the everyday activity it affects, identify the specific difficulty (the friction), and develop an intervention from there.

Patient Case: Jodie

Jodie is a 55-year-old woman living independently with Parkinson's disease. She is employed, socially active, and independent in daily life, but increasing mobility challenges make routine activities harder: bed mobility, dressing, maintaining balance, carrying objects while using mobility aids, freezing of gait, navigating crowded spaces, and moving between sitting and standing. She spends more time planning and adapting her movements, and sometimes avoids activities altogether.

Her case shows that mobility goes beyond walking. It includes everyday transitions, independence, and participation in the community.

Reframing Functional Independence

  • Mobility is the ability to move safely, confidently, and independently through daily life, not only to get from Point A to Point B.
  • Independence does not mean doing everything without help. It means having the ability, choice, and opportunity to take part in meaningful activities.
  • Focus on the patient's lived experience, not just clinical symptoms.
  • Consider how the person, the activity, and the environment interact.

Frugal Innovation & Systems Thinking

Innovation does not have to be technologically complex to be meaningful. Strong solutions prioritize:

  • Affordability and accessibility
  • Durability and maintainability
  • Usability and safety
  • Practicality in real-world environments
